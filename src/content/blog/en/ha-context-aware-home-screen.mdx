My first HA home screen had 40-something cards. Every time I opened it I'd scan past the docked vacuum, the off lights, the locked car, the zero-activity motion sensor. None of that is information. It's just confirmation that nothing unusual happened. Noise dressed up as data.

I started removing everything that was only saying "still fine."

The screenshot above is from this morning. The dishwasher is running (blue card). The car charger is connected, so "Unlock Cable" is sitting next to "Klima Boost". Home status is empty: no lights on, no robots moving. Energy says cheapest now. That's it. Four active things, shown. Everything else, hidden.

## The alert ticker

At the very top sits a `custom:alert-ticker-card` (HACS) with `show_when_clear: false`. When nothing needs attention, it doesn't exist. When something does, it rotates through alerts in priority order:

```yaml
type: custom:alert-ticker-card
show_when_clear: false
cycle_interval: 6
cycle_animation: fade
alerts:
  - entity: binary_sensor.sentinel_person
    state: "on"
    message: "👤 Person detected at Sentinel"
    theme: warning
    priority: 1
    tap_action:
      action: navigate
      navigation_path: /nyt-mobil/overvagning
  - entity: sensor.affalddk_your_address_next_pickup
    operator: "=="
    state: "1"
    message: "🗑️ Waste collected tomorrow"
    theme: warning
    priority: 3
  - entity: lawn_mower.flora
    state: error
    message: "⚠️ Flora error"
    theme: emergency
    priority: 1
```

Person detection is priority 1. Waste tomorrow is priority 3. They cycle every 6 seconds. The ticker tapping on a person alert navigates directly to the security view. When everything is fine the space is just gone. No "All clear" message, no empty bar. Nothing.

## The dishwasher has three states

This took a while to get right. The dishwasher card has three completely different behaviours depending on state:

**Hidden:** when the machine is off or finished. No card, no section.

**Ready:** when `OperationState` is `Ready` AND remote start is allowed. Shows a tile with "Dishwasher ready, set finish time" that links to the appliances view. The idea: you've loaded it, you haven't started it yet, you want to set a delayed start. The card only appears in that exact window.

**Running:** when `OperationState` is `Run`. A different card with a blue icon that shows the finish time ("Dishwasher done 18:30") and how far along it is. No action needed, just awareness.

```yaml
- type: conditional
  conditions:
    - entity: sensor.dishwasher_operation_state
      state: BSH.Common.EnumType.OperationState.Run
  card:
    type: custom:mushroom-template-card
    entity: sensor.dishwasher_operation_state
    name: Dishwasher running
    icon_color: blue
```

Three states, three different cards, zero redundancy.

## Lights: two tiers, one principle

Lights follow a two-layer pattern. Both layers are conditional on something being on.

**Layer 1 (Turn All Lights Off):** When any light or outdoor plug is on, an amber card appears in the left column, under the car, with the count and a single tap to kill everything. These use `custom:mushroom-template-card` (HACS, Mushroom suite) with `card_mod` (HACS) for the tinted backgrounds:

```yaml
- type: conditional
  conditions:
    - condition: or
      conditions:
        - condition: state
          entity: light.ceiling_1
          state: "on"
        - condition: state
          entity: light.smart_flood_lights
          state: "on"
        # ... all lights
  card:
    type: custom:mushroom-template-card
    primary: Turn All Lights Off
    secondary: >
      {% set l = ['light.ceiling_1','light.ceiling_2','light.corner',
                  'light.flood_lights','light.ground_lights']
         | select('is_state','on') | list | count %}
      {% set c = 1 if is_state('switch.string_lights','on') else 0 %}
      {{ l + c }} lights currently on
    icon: mdi:lightbulb-group
    icon_color: amber
    tap_action:
      action: perform-action
      perform_action: homeassistant.turn_off
      target:
        entity_id:
          - light.ceiling_1
          - light.ceiling_2
          - light.corner
          - light.flood_lights
          - light.ground_lights
          - switch.string_lights
    card_mod:
      style: >
        ha-card { background: rgba(255,152,0,0.10) !important;
        border: 1px solid rgba(255,152,0,0.25) !important; }
```

One tap. No navigation, no confirmation, all off. This is the card I use 90% of the time.

**Layer 2 (Living Room):** When indoor lights specifically are on, the individual mushroom light cards appear further down, with full brightness sliders and colour temperature controls. Not just on/off. If you've left the lights on and you're standing in the room wanting to dim them, the controls are right there without navigating away.

The outdoor lights section works the same way but triggers on sunset OR any outdoor light being on. At noon: invisible. At 21:30: there with individual controls for flood lights, ground lights, and string lights.

The two layers serve different needs. The amber card is for "I'm leaving, kill everything." The Living Room card is for "I'm here and want to adjust." Same conditional logic, different depth of control.

## The Škoda section changes based on context

The car is always shown, with lock status and whether it's home. But what sits below it depends on whether the charger is connected.

**Charger not connected:** one card, "Klima Boost". Pre-condition the car before you leave, one tap.

**Charger connected:** "Unlock Cable" appears as a second card below "Klima Boost". When the cable is physically locked in, you need a way to release it. That button only makes sense when a cable is plugged in. So it only appears then. When the car is away from home, a map card joins the same panel.

```yaml
- type: conditional
  conditions:
    - condition: state
      entity: binary_sensor.garage_el_rey_charging_cable_connected
      state: "on"
  card:
    type: custom:mushroom-template-card
    primary: Unlock Cable
    secondary: Tap to release charger cable
    icon_color: orange
    # ...
```

This is the pattern I ended up using everywhere: don't show controls that aren't relevant to the current state of the device.

## Living room, outdoor, robots: cards that earn their place

Several more cards are conditional. All of them need something to be active before they appear:

- **Living room lights:** only visible when at least one indoor light is on. When it appears, it shows full mushroom light cards with dimmer sliders, not just on/off. You're already there because something is on, so give full control.
- **Outdoor section:** appears when the sun is below the horizon OR any outdoor light is on. At noon on a clear day: gone. At 21:30 or if someone turned on the flood lights: there.
- **Roborock:** only when not docked and not idle. Cleaning, returning, paused, error: show it. Sitting in its dock: irrelevant.
- **Flora:** same logic. Mowing, returning or in error: show status and tap to the mower view. Parked: nothing.

The result: on a typical quiet afternoon none of these cards is on screen. When I come home at 22:00 and the outdoor lights are still on, that section is there with one-tap off buttons.

## Two cards that check before they show up

The Quick actions card has a condition of its own. It appears when the dishwasher is ready or running, when the washing machine is programmed, running or finished, when a bathroom fan is on, or when the TV is playing. If none of that is true, the whole card is gone, title included. When it does show, each row has its own condition too, so it only lists what applies.

![Today's Home view: a waste warning in the alert ticker, the Quick actions card with the dishwasher, the TV and the pause light, and the amber Turn All Lights Off card](/blog-images/home-screen-conditional-cards.webp)

On the screenshot it is on because the dishwasher is loaded and the TV is playing Prime Video. The TV adds two rows: one that pauses and plays, and one called "Pause light" that brings the lights up for a break and dims them again with a second tap. Nobody has to open the appliances view or find the remote.

The other card I rely on is the banner at the very top. Each of the three bin types has two alerts in the ticker, one for the day before pickup and one for two days before. The banner in the screenshot says "Plastic & cartons collected tomorrow". Waste has priority 3, so a person at the door or a mower error still goes first. On every other day the banner doesn't exist.

## The length of the screen is the signal

This is the thing I didn't expect. After a few weeks I realised I was reading the screen length before I read the content. Short screen: quiet day, nothing needs attention. Long screen: something is going on, so scroll to find out what.

The waste card is the one exception to the hiding rule. It is always on screen and lists the next pickups, sorted by date. The nudge itself lives in the alert ticker: each of the three bin types adds a warning banner the day before pickup and again two days before, so the banner is only there when the bins need to go out. I haven't forgotten to put them out since I set this up.

The energy card is always there, with the current price and when the next cheap window is. That one earned permanent status because it actually changes what I do. I time the dishwasher and dryer off it.

## What never shows up

Nothing for "everything is fine". No card that says the car is home and locked. No card that says no motion detected. No confirmation that the robot is docked. Those aren't things you need to be told. The absence of a card tells you that.

That's the part that took longest to trust. When the screen is short, the instinct is to wonder if something broke. It didn't. Short means quiet.
