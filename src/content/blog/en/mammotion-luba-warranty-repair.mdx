On Saturday 17 October, Flora stopped doing anything at all. She'd power on and go straight into an error state. The app said "continue working", then "task paused" a second later, over and over. Manual control from the app didn't work either.

Flora is my Mammotion LUBA mini AWD. I've written about [running her from Home Assistant](/en/blog/mammotion-flora-ha-integration/), and none of that helps when the mower won't move. My guess was the left front wheel. It was only a guess.

## A weekend reply

I had already sent a log from the app. July from Mammotion support answered at 08:43 that Saturday, and my reply went out 17 minutes later. By lunchtime she had decided Flora needed to go to the service centre.

That decision depends on where you bought the mower. Mammotion repairs units bought directly from its own store or its official Amazon stores. Dealer purchases get troubleshooting help, and the dealer handles the repair. I bought Flora on mammotion.com, so this was Mammotion's job from start to finish. I paid for her myself, and nobody at Mammotion asked me to write this.

I no longer had the original box, so they offered to send one. Then I made the only mess of the whole case. They asked for a screenshot of my Mammotion ID, and on Sunday I sent the wrong screen. Twice. It took a set of pictures showing exactly where to tap before I got it right.

On Monday I had a repair order and a prepaid UPS label. On Wednesday the box left their warehouse with DHL.

## Two weeks of logistics

I dropped Flora off at UPS on Sunday 25 October. Hedy confirmed it the same afternoon.

After that Lynn kept me posted without me asking. First whether I'd shipped yet, then that the parcel had arrived but still had to be logged in. On 4 November she wrote that Flora had been moved up the repair queue, even though it's peak season.

The service report shows how the time was actually spent. Flora was logged in at the service centre on 2 November at 16:09. The repair was finished on 9 November at 20:51. One week on the bench. Everything else was boxes, labels and lorries.

## What the service report says

With Flora came a printed service report, and it answers the question none of the emails did: what was actually wrong.

![Mammotion's final service report for Flora: front wheel motor and cutting plate replaced under warranty](/blog-images/mammotion-service-report.webp)

Two faults: a front wheel motor defect, which matches my guess, and a cutting plate defect I had never mentioned. They replaced a 410 mm wheel hub motor and the cutting disc. Both are marked "Covered by Warranty", and the amount payable is EUR 0.00.

The cutting disc is the part I keep thinking about. I sent in a mower that wouldn't drive, and they also found and fixed something I hadn't noticed. The report says she passed a hardware diagnostic and an automated run test before she was approved for return.

## Five people, one story

July, Caden, Lynn, Cora and Hedy all handled the case at some point, weekends included. None of them asked me to explain the fault twice. Halfway through I wrote to tell them their communication was amazing. I meant it.

To be fair about it: this is Flora's second hardware fault. The first was a Vision Module that took in moisture during winter storage, which I described in the [Home Assistant post](/en/blog/mammotion-flora-ha-integration/). Two faults are two faults. Both times, though, the fix came faster than I expected and without a single argument about warranty.

Flora arrived back on 13 November, the day after the repair email and 27 days after July's first reply. That email ended with a checklist for unpacking: safety key in, check the LoRa RTK ID, check the screws, and test her indoors if it's cold. Whoever wrote it knows what tends to go wrong after a repair.

Mammotion support, thank you. I wish every support case went like this one.
