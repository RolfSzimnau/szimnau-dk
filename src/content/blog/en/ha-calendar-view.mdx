We had a wall calendar in the kitchen for years. One of those big paper ones with a column per family member. Someone would write something in it, someone else wouldn't see it, and we'd end up with a collision at school pickup or a birthday forgotten until the morning of. The calendar worked fine as long as everyone updated it, which they didn't, reliably.

The HA calendar view didn't replace the habit problem. But it put the same information somewhere everyone already looks.

## The setup

Each family member has a Google Calendar. HA's Google Calendar integration pulls all of them in as separate `calendar.*` entities. The Danish public holidays are a calendar too, and waste collection comes from the `affalddk` integration (HACS), a Danish municipal waste API that creates its own calendar entity with pickup dates.

Everything goes through one card, `custom:calendar-card-pro` (HACS). It has three layouts (list, columns and grid), and the same entity list works in all of them. On the phone I use the list:

```yaml
type: custom:calendar-card-pro
days_to_show: 7
compact_mode: true
split_multiday_events: true
allday_badge: title
show_location: true
show_event_progress_bar: true
weather:
  entity: weather.forecast_home
  position: date
entities:
  - entity: calendar.rolf
    color: "#00bcd4"
    accent_color: "#00bcd4"
  - entity: calendar.family_member_1
    color: "#e040fb"
    accent_color: "#e040fb"
  - entity: calendar.family_member_2
    color: "#ff4081"
    accent_color: "#ff4081"
  - entity: calendar.family_member_3
    color: "#ff7043"
    accent_color: "#ff7043"
  - entity: calendar.holidays_in_denmark
    color: "#fdd835"
    accent_color: "#fdd835"
  - entity: calendar.affalddk_your_address
    color: "#4caf50"
    accent_color: "#4caf50"
```

A few of these settings do more than they look like:

- `days_to_show: 7` loads a week from today.
- `split_multiday_events` shows a trip or a holiday on every day it covers instead of only on the first.
- `allday_badge: title` draws all-day events as a coloured pill around the title, so they don't look like timed appointments.
- `weather` puts a small forecast icon and temperature beside each date. It's handy for working out whether an outdoor plan will survive Thursday.

## The colour coding

Each person gets one colour, consistently. It's the same colour in every context where their events appear. After a few weeks you stop reading the name column and just read the colour. A glance at Monday tells you whose day it is without your eyes needing to focus on text.

I got this slightly wrong at first. In the card, `color` only sets the colour of the event title. The bar beside an event, its tinted row and the all-day pill use a second setting, `accent_color`, and that falls back to the theme's blue. I had set only `color`, so one person's all-day events showed up as blue pills with orange text. Setting `accent_color` to the same hex fixed it. If you colour events per person, set both.

Waste collection is green and the public holidays are yellow. They sit in the same list without needing a person's colour.

## What the waste calendar does

The `affalddk` integration creates calendar events for each pickup type: general waste, recycling, paper, glass. They show up as green entries in the view. On the home screen the waste card only appears within three days of a pickup, but here in the dedicated calendar view, you see the full schedule months out.

That sounds minor. It isn't. Knowing cardboard pickup is next Thursday means I actually break down boxes in time instead of cramming them in the bin the night before.

## A week grid on the wall tablet

I used to skip the grid. I had tried a month view, and it looked correct, but I used it the way I'd used the paper calendar: not often enough, and only to check a specific date. The list was what I used.

On the wall tablet the grid turned out to be the better layout. There's room for seven day columns with the hours down the side, and overlapping appointments are easy to spot.

```yaml
type: custom:calendar-card-pro
view: grid
days_to_show: 7
time_grid:
  start_time: "07:00"
  end_time: "22:00"
  hour_height: 31px
toolbar:
  show: true
  view_switcher: true
  search: true
day_tap_action:
  action: add-event
# entities: the same list as above
```

![Home Assistant calendar card in grid layout on a wall tablet: seven day columns from 07:00 to 22:00, all-day events in a band above the hours and a coloured legend below](/blog-images/ha-calendar-view-grid-tablet.webp)

`time_grid.start_time` and `end_time` cut the night out of the grid. `hour_height` is the one to tune. The default is 48 px, which makes a day taller than a 1280×800 screen. At 46 px the evening was still off screen, so I went down until the whole week fit without scrolling. For me that was 31 px, and I checked it with a screenshot at the tablet's size. All-day events sit in a band above the hours and don't take space from the grid.

The toolbar adds three things. A switcher lets anyone flip to the list or columns layout, and each device remembers its own choice. A refresh button reloads the calendars. A search button looks for events a year back and a year ahead. The card also keeps the weather forecast on each day header.

## Adding events from the card

`day_tap_action: add-event` turns a tap on a day into a new event. In the list layout you tap the date or the empty space between events. In the grid you tap an empty spot, and the event starts at that quarter hour. You can also hold and drag across several hours to set the length. A tap on an existing event does something else, so it never creates a second one by mistake.

![The Home Assistant calendar card's new-event form open on the tablet: summary, start and end time, location, calendar and description, with a new event drawn on Wednesday](/blog-images/ha-calendar-view-add-event-tablet.webp)

The form has a summary, a start and end time, a location, a calendar and a description. It starts on the first calendar in the list that accepts new events, which is mine, and you can change it before saving.

This works with Google, with limits. The card's documentation lists Google Calendar as add: yes, delete: yes, edit: no. Home Assistant's Google integration can create and delete events but not change them, so the card's edit fields and drag-to-move don't apply to our calendars. That's also why I haven't set `event_tap_action: details`. It would open a form on existing events, and on a wall tablet I don't want a tap to be able to delete one.

One thing tripped me up. I had set `allow_event_edit: false` and `allow_event_delete: false` to be safe. After that, tapping a day did nothing on the phone. I removed both options and reloaded, and the form opened. I can't say whether the options or a cached card was the cause, but with `event_tap_action` left unset they protect nothing, so I'd leave them out.

## What I still don't use

I don't use a month view or week numbers. And I don't edit events from the dashboard, because Google doesn't let HA do it.

The home screen shows a compact list of the next 5 events from all calendars combined, and a tap opens the calendar page. The calendar page is the list on the phone and the grid on the tablet. When I want to plan a week out I go there. Day-to-day I just look at the home screen. Several levels of zoom off the same data.
